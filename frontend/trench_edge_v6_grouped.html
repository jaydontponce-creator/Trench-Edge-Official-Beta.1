How Trench Edge Works

A plain-language guide to what the model does, how it forms predictions, and how to use the output responsibly.

What is Trench Edge?

Trench Edge is a college-football prediction and market-analysis model. It estimates each team's scoring environment, compares that projection with the sportsbook market, and then decides whether the difference is large and reliable enough to become a qualified wager.

What does the model consider?

TE combines offensive and defensive efficiency, passing and rushing performance, offensive-line and defensive-front matchups, pace, explosiveness, first-down creation, turnovers, special teams, home field, weather, roster continuity, injuries, and opponent context. The technical calculations remain available in Advanced Analytics, but they are translated into plain language everywhere else.

How are picks made?

First, TE projects a score. Second, it compares that score with the current spread and total. Third, it checks whether the underlying data are reliable. Fourth, it tests matchup and game-volatility risks. Finally, it estimates whether the probability of winning the wager is high enough to overcome the sportsbook's price. A game can have a strong prediction and still be a PASS if the price is not attractive.

What does “market advantage” mean?

The sportsbook sets a price. TE independently estimates the game. The difference between those two views is potential betting value, but a difference alone is not enough. The model requires sufficient data quality, risk controls, and positive expected value before qualifying a bet.

Why does risk matter?

Risk is the possibility that factors not fully reflected in the central projection cause the game to behave differently than expected. Examples include quarterback pressure creating turnovers, an offensive line collapsing on passing downs, stalled red-zone drives, special-teams mistakes, short fields, weather changes, injuries, unusual game scripts, or a favorite taking its foot off the gas. Risk does not mean the prediction is wrong. It measures how vulnerable the prediction is to plausible ways the game can break away from its expected path.

Predictions are not guarantees

TE is designed to improve decision quality, not guarantee outcomes. Betting markets contain uncertainty and variance. The most useful output is often a PASS because protecting capital when the edge is unclear is part of the model's purpose.
